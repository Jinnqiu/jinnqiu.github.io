Puzzles for Kids

Page 1 · 15 puzzles for curious minds aged 11–16.

Start with a warm-up or jump to a challenge. Try an idea, change your mind, and ask why it works. Some puzzles have more than one answer.

Use the boards below or a pencil and paper. Each puzzle has a hint and a worked solution to open when you are ready. There is no timer.

Warm-ups

01 · Routes

The garden patrol

Start at S and take your first step to the right. Move only between squares sharing an edge. Visit every open square exactly once, finishing at F. The square marked × is blocked.

Choose the squares in your route, starting with S. Use Undo to take back a step.

Hint

The top-right corner has only two neighbours. Your route must connect it to both.

Solution and why

Follow the numbers in this grid, from 1 to 15:

 1   2   3   4
10   9   ×   5
11   8   7   6
12  13  14  15

The first step and the corners force the route across the top and down the right. You cannot finish early. The square just left of F must be saved for the last step, which forces the detour through the middle and left side.

02 · Numbers

Six seats, three sums

Use each number from 1 to 6 exactly once. The columns must total 6, 7 and 8, from left to right. The top row must total 10, and the top-left number must be even.

Total 6Total 7Total 8

Hint

Which pair can make 6 with an even number on top? Then look at the numbers left for the other columns.

Solution and why
4  1  5
2  6  3

The first column must contain 2 and 4. The last then needs 3 and 5, leaving 1 and 6 in the middle. Putting 2 at the top left would require the other two top numbers to total 8, which is impossible with these pairs. Putting 4 there forces 1 and 5 beside it.

03 · Symmetry

The hidden badge

Shade squares so the design is unchanged by both a left–right reflection and a top–bottom reflection. The numbers beside the board give the shaded-square totals for each row and column.

Choose a square to shade or unshade it.

1232112321

Hint

A row with just one shaded square must put it in the centre. Apply the same idea to the columns.

Solution and why
· · ■ · ·
· ■ · ■ ·
■ · ■ · ■
· ■ · ■ ·
· · ■ · ·

The outside rows and columns force the four tips. The middle row needs its centre shaded too. The centre column is now full, so rows 2 and 4 must use columns 2 and 4. Both reflections and all ten totals then agree.

04 · Numbers

Triangle totals

Place the numbers 1 to 6 around the triangle, using each once. The three numbers along each side must add to 10. Corner numbers belong to two sides.

Hint

Adding the three side totals counts each corner twice and each midpoint once. What must the three corners add to?

Solution and why

Put 1 at the top, 3 at the bottom left and 5 at the bottom right. The left midpoint is 6, the right midpoint is 4, and the bottom midpoint is 2.

The side totals are 1 + 6 + 3, 1 + 4 + 5, and 3 + 2 + 5: all 10. The numbers 1 to 6 total 21, while the three sides total 30, so the corners must total 9. Rotations and reflections work too.

05 · Logic

The woodland queue

Place Hare, Bear, Fox, Frog and Owl once each, from left to right.

  • Owl is immediately to Frog’s right.
  • Exactly one animal stands between Fox and Hare.
  • Bear is somewhere to Hare’s right.
  • Neither Frog nor Bear is at either end.

Hint

Treat Frog and Owl as a two-animal block. Try each possible place for the block.

Solution and why

Hare → Bear → Fox → Frog → Owl.

If Frog were second or third, the other clues would force Hare and Fox to the two ends, breaking their spacing rule. So Frog is fourth and Owl fifth. Bear must be second, between Hare and Fox, and Bear’s clue puts Hare first.

06 · Deduction

A small Sudoku

Fill each empty square with a number from 1 to 4. Every row, every column and each outlined 2-by-2 box must contain all four numbers exactly once.

Fill the empty squares
14
41
24
31

Hint

Look at the bottom-left box. Which numbers are missing, and which can go in its top-right square?

Solution and why
1  2 │ 3  4
3  4 │ 1  2
─────┼─────
2  1 │ 4  3
4  3 │ 2  1

The bottom-left box is missing 1 and 4. Row 3 already has 4, so its second square is 1; the bottom-left square is 4. The remaining entries follow from the row, column and box rules. This puzzle has one solution.

07 · Working backward

Double the destination

There are 2 tokens in bowl A, 3 in B and 7 in C. Make exactly two moves to leave 4 tokens in every bowl.

In each move, transfer exactly as many tokens as the destination already holds, doubling its count. The source must have enough tokens, and the destination must not be empty.

A 2

B 3

C 7

Moves: 0 of 2

Hint

Just before the last move, the destination must hold 2 tokens, the source 6, and the untouched bowl 4.

Solution and why

First move 3 tokens from C to B, giving A: 2, B: 6, C: 4. Then move 2 from B to A, giving 4 in every bowl.

Working backward from the target tells you to look for counts 2, 6 and 4 after one move. Only the first move above produces those counts from the starting position.

08 · Placement

Four quiet owls

Place four owls on this board. No two may share a row, a column or a diagonal of any length. Diagonal means a straight line through diagonally touching squares.

Choose a square to place or remove an owl.

Hint

Try putting the first owl in row 1, column 2. Which squares are still available in the next row?

Solution and why

Use columns 2, 4, 1, 3 in rows 1, 2, 3, 4 respectively. No pair shares a row, column or diagonal.

Further challenge: find every solution. The only other one is its mirror image: columns 3, 1, 4, 2. Trying each possible first-row column, and ruling out attacked squares, exhausts the possibilities.

09 · Planning

Measure four litres

You have empty 3-litre and 5-litre jugs, a tap and a drain. Get exactly 4 litres in the 5-litre jug.

A move fills a jug completely, empties it completely, or pours from one jug to the other until the source is empty or the destination is full. There are no measuring marks.

0 L

3-litre jug

0 L

5-litre jug

Moves: 0

Hint

Fill the big jug and pour into the small one. Can you make use of the 2 litres left over?

Solution and why
  1. Fill the 5-litre jug.
  2. Pour into the 3-litre jug; 2 litres remain in the big jug.
  3. Empty the small jug.
  4. Pour the 2 litres into the small jug.
  5. Fill the big jug again.
  6. Top up the small jug with 1 litre, leaving 4 litres in the big jug.

That takes six moves. Further challenge: can you prove that five moves cannot be enough? List the reachable pairs of amounts, one move at a time, discarding pairs you have seen before; 4 litres first appears at move six.

10 · Counting

How many routes?

Travel from S to F, following the streets. You may move only right or down, one street segment at a time. You cannot pass through the dot marked ×.

How many different routes are possible? Two routes are different if their sequences of moves differ.

Hint

Write each route as a word made of R and D. Every complete route has three R’s and two D’s.

Solution and why

There are 4 routes: DDRRR, RRDDR, RRDRD and RRRDD.

Without the closed dot, choosing the two down-steps among five moves gives 10 routes. There are 2 ways to reach the closed dot and 3 ways to continue from it, so 6 routes use it. Removing those leaves 4.

Challenges

11 · Networks

The one-stroke house

Start at B. Follow every road exactly once and finish at D. You may revisit a point, but you cannot reuse a road.

B is already selected. Choose the next point on your route.

Roads: A–B, A–C, B–D, C–D, C–E, D–E and B–C. Crossings are not extra stopping points.

Route: B

Hint

Count the roads meeting at each point. Why do the two points with odd counts matter?

Solution and why

One route is B → A → C → E → D → C → B → D.

Each intermediate visit to a point uses an arrival and a departure. Only the start and finish can have an unpaired road. Here B and D each have three roads, while A and E have two and C has four. This explains the required endpoints.

12 · Seeing structure

Remove two sticks

The picture contains four small squares and one large square. Remove exactly two sticks so that exactly one small square and one large square remain. Count squares of both sizes; do not move the remaining sticks.

Choose a stick to remove it; choose it again to put it back.

Hint

Keeping the large square means keeping its entire outside border. Which inside sticks can you remove?

Solution and why

Remove the left half of the middle horizontal line and the upper half of the middle vertical line. Only the bottom-right small square and the large outside square remain.

Choose one of the two inner horizontal sticks and one of the two inner vertical sticks. Each such pair breaks three small squares and preserves the outside border, giving four solutions.

13 · Information

The heavier pebble

Nine pebbles look identical. Exactly one is heavier; the other eight weigh the same. Identify the heavier pebble using a balance scale twice.

Put equal numbers of pebbles on the two pans. The scale tells you which pan is heavier, or whether they balance. Reset starts a new round. Check your answer only after making two weighings.

Weighings: 0 of 2

    Hint

    Divide the pebbles into three groups of three. A balance has three possible outcomes.

    Solution and why

    Weigh pebbles 1–3 against 4–6. If one pan is heavier, its group contains the mystery pebble; if they balance, the mystery pebble is among 7–9.

    From the suspect group, weigh one pebble against another. The heavier one is the answer; if they balance, it is the third pebble.

    Why two weighings suffice: each result chooses among three possibilities. Two weighings can distinguish nine possibilities, exactly the number of pebbles. One weighing cannot guarantee an answer.

    14 · Planning and proof

    The arrow swap

    Swap the two groups: finish with three ◀ arrows on the left, the empty cell in the middle, and three ▶ arrows on the right.

    Each arrow moves only in the direction it points. It may slide into the next cell if empty, or jump over one arrow pointing the other way into an empty cell. No other jumps are allowed.

    Choose an arrow to move it. If you get stuck, reset and try another plan.

    Moves: 0

    Hint

    Begin by moving the right arrow nearest the gap. Alternate short runs of jumps with single slides.

    Solution and why

    Number the cells 1–7 from left to right. Move the arrow currently in each of these cells:

    3, 5, 6, 4, 2, 1, 3, 5, 7, 6, 4, 2, 3, 5, 4.

    That is 15 moves. Each of the three right arrows must cross each of the three left arrows, requiring nine jumps. The arrows must travel 24 cell-lengths in total. The nine jumps account for 18, so six single-cell slides are also needed. Thus every successful solution uses 15 moves.

    15 · An impossibility proof

    Two missing corners

    Remove the top-left and bottom-right squares from a 4-by-4 board. Can you cover the remaining 14 squares with seven dominoes?

    Each domino covers exactly two squares sharing an edge. You may rotate dominoes, but they must not overlap or extend beyond the board. Try to explain your answer.

    ××
    Can it be covered?

    Hint

    Colour the board like a chessboard. How many light and dark squares remain? What does each domino cover?

    Solution and why

    No. The removed corners have the same colour. There are 6 squares of that colour left and 8 of the other.

    Every domino covers one square of each colour. Seven dominoes would therefore need 7 of each, which this board does not have. This rules out every possible arrangement at once.

    Further challenge: does this argument still work for an 8-by-8 board with opposite corners removed? Yes: it leaves 30 squares of one colour and 32 of the other.
